Where the expertise comes from

The full evidence-based medicine cycle — lived, practiced, and now built into AI.

Philosophy
Trust has to be built where the domain knowledge lives.

Rooted in medicine — not a hammer looking for nails.

My approach to clinical AI grows out of my history across the entire evidence-based medicine lifecycle. I know exactly where clinical knowledge breaks down — and I’m driven to lift the unbearable burden of the clinical knowledge explosion from the people who carry it every day.

My Path
My version of an unconventional path
Roots
Proofs and arts.

I grew up competitive in math and science, and in love with art — drawing, singing, the stage. What tied them together was a love of innovation. Once, I designed a stage show to bring Eastern Asian calligraphy to life through chemistry — a hair dryer wrote the script with heat, and erased it with cool air.

Medicine
Medicine was meaningful enough.

A proof holds inside a closed world. Medicine mattered more — and its knowledge operating in an open world drew me in as a complex system: evidence that keeps evolving, exceptions that change everything, logic that rarely stays clean.

The spark
Then, the boiling point.

As clinical knowledge grew faster than anyone could carry it, the work of Yuval Noah Harari gave me my aha moment — years before large language models arrived: AI was going to radically change how medicine is practiced.

Today
Now: neurosymbolic.

I didn’t guess at the solution; I went into health informatics and the neurosymbolic path. Having lived, practiced, and implemented the full evidence-based medicine cycle, I now build the guardrails it demands: logic and language models keeping each other honest in production.

The craft
Complex inside, simple to use.

I walked into clinical medicine and formal reasoning because they matter — never intimidated by how complicated they are. Like the calligraphy appearing under a hair dryer, the chemistry stays backstage. I hide hardcore informatics behind intuitive design — so domain experts take the center stage.

AI fails not for lack of data, but for lack of understanding.

Philosophy — the questions it asks, the ontologies it clarifies — is how we fix it. That’s my passion — and I make it approachable. As MIT Sloan put it: ‘Philosophy eats AI.’

In medicine, that philosophy is the deep, hard-won domain expertise we trust.
