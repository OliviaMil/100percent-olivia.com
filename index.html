Where the expertise comes from

The full evidence-based medicine cycle — lived, practiced, and now built into AI.

Philosophy
Trust has to be built where the knowledge lives.

Rooted in medicine — not a hammer looking for nails.

My approach to clinical AI grows out of my history across the entire evidence-based medicine lifecycle. I know exactly where clinical knowledge breaks down — and I’m driven to reduce its burden, safely.

My Path
My version of an unconventional path
Roots
Proofs and pictures.

I grew up competitive in math and STEM, and in love with art — drawing, calligraphy, the stage. What tied them together was a love of innovation. Once, I brought live calligraphy onto the stage in a chemistry magic show I designed — where a hair dryer did the writing.

Medicine
Medicine was meaningful enough.

A proof holds inside a closed world. Medicine mattered more — and its knowledge drew me in as a complex system: evidence that keeps evolving, exceptions that change everything, logic that rarely stays clean.

The spark
Then, the boiling point.

As clinical knowledge grew faster than anyone could carry it, the work of Yuval Noah Harari gave me my aha moment — years before large language models arrived: AI was going to radically change how medicine is practiced.

The choice
Symbolic or neural?

I didn’t guess. I went deep into health informatics and hardcore explainable AI — and chose the symbolic path.

Today
Now: neurosymbolic.

I come from the full evidence-based medicine cycle — lived and practiced — so I build what it takes to guardrail medicine’s safety and rigor: logic and language models, each keeping the other honest — in production, not just in demos.

The drive
Undaunted by complexity.

I walked into clinical medicine and formal reasoning because they matter — never intimidated by how complicated they are. And I’m driven to lift the unbearable burden of the clinical knowledge explosion from the people who carry it every day.

The craft
Complex inside, simple to use.

This is where art and innovation come back in. Like calligraphy appearing under a hair dryer, the chemistry stays backstage: I hide the complicated parts behind design that feels simple — so clinicians and end users get rigor they can actually use.

AI fails not for lack of data, but for lack of understanding.

Philosophy — the questions it asks, the ontologies it clarifies — is how we fix it. That’s my passion — and I make it approachable. As MIT Sloan put it: ‘Philosophy eats AI.’
